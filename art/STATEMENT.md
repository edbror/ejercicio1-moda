# Artist Statement

*on* **Drift** *(2026)*

---

I wanted to make something I could not finish, and then could not control.

For years my work circled the same discomfort: the artist as origin, the
composer as sovereign, the gallery as a place where one will is broadcast to
many. I no longer believe in that arrangement. Most of what shapes a life now
happens inside systems too large and too slow to command — the climate, the
network, the market, the weather of attention itself. We act inside them. Our
actions are real. They are never decisive. I wanted an instrument that told the
truth about that.

So *Drift* has no recordings in it. I did not want to hand you a frozen
decision I made in the past and call it music. Everything you hear is computed
in the instant of listening and will never happen the same way twice — even the
room the sounds seem to stand in is synthesised from noise, a space that does
not exist. The piece is not a document of a process. It is the process, still
going, whether or not you are there.

I gave you sliders, and then I made them lie a little. They register your
*wish*, not your command; the system folds your wish into its own slow
wandering and quietly pulls everything back toward a balance you never chose. I
made that visible on purpose — a small light on each control shows where the
value really went, so you can watch the distance between what you wanted and
what happened, opening and closing, all night. This is the most honest thing in
the work. You are not steering. You are leaning. It leans back.

And then I gave the deepest decision — the key, the tonal ground everything
stands on — to the Sun. When you press *Cosmos*, the instrument stops inventing
its own weather and listens instead to the actual solar wind, read by
spacecraft parked at the L1 point, a still place a million and a half kilometres
out where the Earth's gravity and the Sun's cancel each other. The data reaches
us already an hour old. When the Sun's magnetic field turns southward — the
same event that lights the aurora — the harmony darkens. A geomagnetic storm
does not influence the music. It re-keys it, without asking. I find that
beautiful and a little frightening, which is the register I was after.

I am not the composer of this piece. A star is, and it has never heard of
music. I am something smaller and, I think, more truthful: a tender of
conditions. I set the rules and then I let go.

You may nudge it. You will never hold it.

That is the work — and, lately, it is also how I have learned to live.

# *Drift* (2026)
## generative sound instrument · live Web Audio synthesis · real-time heliophysical telemetry

---

**Synopsis**

*Drift* is a self-evolving sound environment that asks what is left of
authorship once the author admits they are not in charge. Nothing in the work
is recorded. Every grain of sound — each bell struck into being by frequency
modulation, each pad swelling beneath a moving filter, even the reverberant
space the sounds appear to inhabit — is synthesised the instant you hear it and
will never recur in that exact form. The piece has no score, no loop, no end. It
is not a recording of a process; it *is* the process, ongoing, indifferent to
whether anyone is listening.

At its centre sits a quiet provocation about control. The interface offers you
seven sliders and invites you to shape the weather of the sound. But the
instrument does not obey. Your gesture is registered as a *wish*, not a command;
the system blends it with its own slow, autonomous wandering and a
mean-reverting drift that pulls everything back toward an equilibrium you never
set. A luminous marker on each control shows where the value has *actually*
gone — so the work renders your powerlessness visible, second by second, as the
gap between intention and result. You are not the composer. You are weather
pressure. You lean on the system, and it leans back.

The piece then performs its most radical refusal of human authority: it hands
the tonality of the music to the Sun. In its synced state, *Drift* abandons its
internal weather and tunes itself to **live space-weather telemetry** — the
solar wind, the interplanetary magnetic field, the geomagnetic storm index — as
measured by spacecraft stationed at the **L1 Lagrange point**, a gravitational
null a million and a half kilometres sunward of Earth, where our planet's pull
and the Sun's cancel into stillness. The data is roughly an hour old by the time
it arrives: you are not hearing the present but a message already in transit, a
sound that is always a little behind its cause. When the magnetic field turns
southward — the same physical event that ignites the aurora — the harmony
darkens. A geomagnetic substorm does not *influence* the music; it **re-keys**
it, forcing the work into a new tonal centre without consent. The composer is a
star, ninety-three million miles away, that has never heard of music.

What emerges is a meditation on agency at planetary and cosmic scale. *Drift*
stages the contemporary condition of acting within systems too large, too slow,
and too entangled to command — climate, network, market, sky — where our inputs
are real but never decisive, where we are participants in outcomes we author
only in part. It proposes a humbler aesthetics: not mastery, but **tending**.
Not the artist as origin, but the artist as one pressure among many in a field
that exceeds them. To sit with the piece is to practise a kind of attention that
has no payoff — to influence something that will continue, unbothered and
unrepeatable, long after you close the window, driven by a wind that has been
blowing since before there was anyone to name it.

You may nudge it. You will never hold it. That is the work.

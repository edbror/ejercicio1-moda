Sound Work · 2026

◓ Drift

Generative sound instrument · live Web Audio synthesis · real-time heliophysical telemetry · duration infinite · no recordings used

Drift asks what is left of authorship once the author admits they are not in charge. Nothing here is recorded; every sound is synthesised the instant you hear it and will never recur in that exact form. There is no score, no loop, no end. It is not a recording of a process — it is the process, ongoing, indifferent to whether anyone is listening.

The interface offers you controls and invites you to shape the weather of the sound. But the instrument does not obey. Each gesture is a wish, not a command: the system blends your wish with its own slow wandering and a drift that pulls everything back toward an equilibrium you never set. A glowing marker on each control shows where the value actually went — the gap between intention and result, opening and closing, second by second. You are not the composer. You are weather pressure. You lean on the system, and it leans back.

The work then performs its most radical refusal of human authority: it hands the tonality of the music to the Sun. Synced, Drift tunes itself to live solar-wind and geomagnetic data measured by spacecraft at the L1 Lagrange point, 1.5 million kilometres sunward, where Earth's gravity and the Sun's cancel into stillness. The data arrives an hour old: you hear not the present, but a message already in transit. When the magnetic field turns southward — the same event that lights the aurora — the harmony darkens. A geomagnetic substorm does not influence the music; it re-keys it, without consent. The composer is a star that has never heard of music.

Drift (2026). Generative sound; live Web Audio synthesis; real-time heliophysical telemetry. Audio is synthesised live on the visitor's device — no recordings are used, including the reverberant space, which is generated from noise. Space-weather data courtesy of the NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (DSCOVR / ACE), retrieved live at the L1 Lagrange point; when the feeds are unavailable the instrument continues to generate its own weather. The piece is most fully itself when left alone.  ·  Headphones recommended.
